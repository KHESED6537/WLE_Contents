## Which Is Better: Air or Sea Freight?

You can consider air freight for items you will need immediately after arriving in South Korea, and sea freight for bulky furniture or everyday belongings that you do not need urgently.

| Shipping method | When to consider it | What to check in the quote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Air freight** | Relatively small quantities of clothing, books, and everyday items, or items you need to receive first | Actual weight and volumetric weight; the scope of collection, customs clearance, and delivery |
| **Sea freight: LCL** | Several boxes and some furniture that do not fill an entire container | Volume after packing, minimum freight charges, and destination charges in South Korea |
| **Sea freight: FCL** | A large quantity of belongings from an entire home, including furniture and appliances | Container type; packing, loading and unloading, and final delivery arrangements |

LCL is a method in which you share container space with other shipments, while FCL is a method in which goods are transported on a container basis.

It is difficult to determine which method is more economical from the number of boxes alone. **It is important to compare options using the dimensions and weight after packing, with destination charges included.** You can also consult us about sending urgently needed items by air and the remaining belongings by sea.

Please check the shipping schedule, including collection, loading for transport, customs clearance, and final delivery. The estimated arrival date may change depending on flight or vessel schedules and local handling conditions.

## Are Return-Home Belongings Exempt from Customs Duty and VAT?

Even previously used household goods are not automatically exempt simply because they are being shipped as return-home belongings. **Customs makes its assessment based on factors such as the period of residence, whether family members accompany you, how long the items have been used, and their quantities.**

For South Korean nationals in general, the categories are as follows.

| Category | General overseas residence requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Person relocating (이사자) | Having a residence overseas and living there for at least one year |
| Person relocating with accompanying family (가족을 동반한 이사자) | Having a residence overseas and living there for at least six months |
| Short-term resident (단기체류자) | Residence of at least three months but less than one year |
| Short-term resident with accompanying family (가족을 동반한 단기체류자) | Residence of at least three months but less than six months |

Detailed conditions apply to the recognition of accompanying family members and the calculation of the residence period. Different criteria, including the intended period of residence in South Korea, apply to foreign nationals and Korean nationals with permanent residence abroad, so please let us know during your consultation.

In general, household goods used for at least three months may qualify for duty and tax exemption, subject to a review of whether they are taxable and other relevant conditions. New products, goods intended for sale, or quantities that are excessive in relation to the size of the household require a separate assessment.

**The timing of the shipment's arrival is also important.** In principle, you should plan for your shipment to arrive within six months of your own entry into South Korea. Please tell us both your expected return date and when you intend to send your belongings.

## Documents Required for Customs Clearance of Household Goods (Some Forms Provided)

| Document | What to prepare |
| --- | --- |
| **Passport copy** | Your passport and, where required, the passports of accompanying family members |
| **Household goods import inventory (이사물품 반입내역서)** | The actual items being shipped and their quantities |
| **Statement of reasons for importing household goods (이사물품 사유서)** | Complete and sign the form provided |
| **Certificate of Entry and Exit (출입국에 관한 사실증명)** | Records that can confirm your period of residence and actual entry history |
| **Box-by-box packing list** | Box number, item description, quantity, and whether the items have been used |
| **Additional supporting documents** | Proof of residence, family relationship documents, identity-related documents, and other evidence where required |

The required documents may vary depending on your nationality, residence history, and whether family members accompany you. We will also explain any power of attorney and transport-related documents in accordance with the applicable procedures.

Numbering your boxes when packing and using the same numbers on your packing list makes it easier to prepare the documents and organise your belongings after arrival.
